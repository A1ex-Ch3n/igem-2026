## Email body

Dear President Dudley,

I am writing to introduce a student initiative at Claremont McKenna that has grown to the point of
needing a home, and to ask whether you might spare twenty to thirty minutes to discuss it.

My name is Alex (Chun-Yu) Chen, and I founded and currently lead iGEM Claremont, our team for the
International Genetically Engineered Machine competition. Founded at MIT, iGEM is now the largest
synthetic biology competition in the world: several hundred university teams each spend a year
designing and building an original biological project, then present it at the Grand Jamboree, where
the work is judged on its science, its engineering, and the responsibility with which it was done.
It is one of the few venues where undergraduate research is held to a genuinely professional
standard. We are ten students, and we are registered for the 2026 season.

This year we have spent on a project that began in Claremont itself. We collected topsoil from the
California Botanic Garden, isolated *Pseudomonas* bacteria from it, and
then isolated a bacteriophage — a virus that infects only bacteria and is harmless to people —
capable of killing one of those strains. Phages are one of the more promising routes to antibiotics
that kill a single target and leave the rest of a patient's microbiome intact, and our work concerns
the phage protein that decides which bacterium it is able to attack.

The work has produced things we can point to. Three complete bacterial genomes are now deposited in
the public sequence archives, and the announcement describing them was published earlier this month
in *Microbiology Resource Announcements*, a journal of the American Society for Microbiology, with an
undergraduate as first author: https://doi.org/10.1128/mra.00793-26. We have also sequenced the
complete genome of the phage, which appears to match nothing previously described, and confirmed
that it carries no virulence genes and no antibiotic-resistance genes — the screen that lets the
project proceed responsibly. Six engineered protein constructs are at the bench now, in expression
testing. All of it has been done under the mentorship of Professor Cesar Ignacio-Espinoza.

What I would like to talk with you about is how this becomes something the College keeps, rather
than something that happened once. Having started the team, I am conscious that at present it runs
entirely on the conviction of the students in it. That has carried us further than I expected, but it
is a fragile foundation: when this group graduates, almost nothing we have built is guaranteed to
outlast us. I would value your thinking on
what it would take to change that — a departmental home with a standing faculty advisor, a recurring
line of support, and access to laboratory space and equipment — so that each incoming class inherits
a working team instead of starting from nothing.

I have copied Professor Ran, Chair of the Kravis Department of Integrated Sciences, who I hope will
be part of that conversation.

I would be glad to meet whenever is easiest for you. On my side, Tuesday and Thursday mornings
before noon are open, as are Friday afternoons between twelve and four; if a weekend is simpler for
your calendar, I can make that work too. I am happy to arrange around whatever your office finds
convenient.

Thank you for your time, and for considering this.

With respect,

Alex (Chun-Yu) Chen
Founder and Team Lead, iGEM Claremont
Claremont McKenna College, Class of [year]

## If you want it shorter

The body runs about 570 words, which is long for a president's inbox. It earns the length only if he
reads the whole thing. Two cuts take it to roughly 400 without losing the case:

- In the iGEM paragraph, drop *"where the work is judged on its science, its engineering, and the
  responsibility with which it was done"* and *"It is one of the few venues where undergraduate
  research is held to a genuinely professional standard."* The Jamboree sentence survives on its own.
- In the results paragraph, drop the phage-genome sentence entirely (the novel genome and the safety
  screen). The published paper plus "six engineered protein constructs are at the bench now" is
  already the strongest pair of facts, and the phage genome is the one a curious president would ask
  about in the room — which is a better place for it than the email.

Do not cut the founder sentence or the continuity paragraph. That is the actual ask.

## Notes on what this draft does and does not say

**Deliberately left out.** The registration fees the three of you paid out of pocket, and any dollar
figure. Per your brief, this reads as an introduction, not an invoice. Note, though, that "a
recurring line of support" does name funding as a meeting topic — which is honesty about the agenda
rather than a request. Omitting money entirely and then raising it in the room would land worse.

**Your six wishes, compressed to three.** You listed a budget line, an official club or departmental
home, a yearly recruitment model, supervision by a named professor, official CMC project status,
lab and equipment access, and a Kravis point of contact. As a list that reads as a wish list, so it
is grouped into a faculty and departmental home with a standing advisor, a recurring line of
support, and lab access. Continuity across cohorts became the *reason* for all three rather than a
fourth item — that is the "each incoming class inherits a working team" clause.

**Accuracy filter.** Every scientific claim above is one that survives the red-lines table in
`2026-08-21_five_minute_talk_script.md` §2, since Prof. Ran is copied and will read it as a
scientist. Asserted: the published paper, the three deposited genomes, the novel phage genome, the
zero-virulence/zero-resistance screen, constructs in expression testing. Not asserted, and not to be
added: that O-antigen differences explain the observed host range (a prediction — the 7/4 assay
readout is still out), that ancestral reconstruction has been done (the PI's tree is in progress),
any protein family assignment for the β-helix, and anything "T7-like." The 0915 gels are
inconclusive, which is why the phrase is "in expression testing" and not "expressed."

**Tone.** Slightly formal, sincere, no hedging and no flattery. The one emotionally direct sentence
is "it runs entirely on the conviction of the students in it," which is where your passion is
carried; if any line sounds unlike you, rewrite that one first and let the rest follow. Your being
the founder is stated once, in that same paragraph, because it is what gives the continuity argument
its force — the person who started the thing is the one worried about it outlasting him.
