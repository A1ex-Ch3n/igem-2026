# PMM (Pseudomonas Minimum Media)

## Purpose
PMM is a defined minimal medium with a phosphate buffer base plus a mineral salt component; it supports *Pseudomonas* growth while restricting many other soil bacteria. It is used as **solid plates** for streak-based isolation of bacteria from soil enrichments, and (in the broader literature) as a **broth** for liquid enrichment.

## Recipe variants

Three sources describe slightly different PMM recipes. The lab uses the **simplified version** (notebook 5-21); the full JMBE recipe is preserved here as reference for future expansion.

### Variant A — Notebook 5-21 (what the team actually made)

Per **1000 mL** PMM solution (autoclave **45 min**, separated into 2 bottles before autoclaving):

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| H₂O | 900 mL |
| Monosodium phosphate (NaH₂PO₄·H₂O) | 2.07 g |
| Dipotassium phosphate (K₂HPO₄) | 2.61 g |
| FeCl₃ | 0.05 g |
| Agar (for solid plates) | 15 g |

- Adjust pH to **6.8** using 1 N NaOH or HCl after dissolving the phosphates.
- The recipe label on the notebook says "following Rafael's notebook" — this is a simplified subset of the JMBE recipe.

### Variant B — `PMM_ProtocoL (1).docx`

Per 1 L final volume, the protocol file in `../PMM_ProtocoL (1).docx` adds further components beyond Variant A: **MgSO₄ 0.5 g**, **CaCl₂ stock 0.5 mL** (from a 1 % w/v stock), and specifies **carbon sources** (sodium succinate 10 % w/v, 10 mL/L) and **NH₄Cl** (20 % w/v, 10 mL/L) added **after autoclave** through 0.22 µm filter sterilization. Autoclave the mineral base for **20 min at 121 °C**.

### Variant C — JMBE PDF (`PseudomonasClassAppendixes.pdf`, p. 2)

Per 1 L: 15 mL of 1 M NaH₂PO₄ + 15 mL of 1 M K₂HPO₄ + 970 mL DI H₂O → confirm pH 6.8 → dissolve 0.5 g MgSO₄ + 0.05 g FeCl₃ + 0.005 g CaCl₂ → autoclave. Carbon sources (sodium benzoate, p-hydroxybenzoate, sodium succinate, histidine, arginine, trehalose, penicillin G) are listed as options; add at **10 mL of 10 % w/v stock per L** after autoclave. NH₄Cl at **10 mL of 20 % w/v per L** if the carbon source does not provide nitrogen.

## Materials & equipment
- 1 L flask (or sized to final volume)
- pH meter or pH paper, 1 N NaOH and 1 N HCl
- Scale
- Autoclave
- Autoclave indicator tape
- Pipette (for dispensing into plates)
- Petri dishes
- UV-light safety cabin (used on 5-22 while plates solidified)

## Reagents
- NaH₂PO₄·H₂O (monosodium phosphate, monohydrate)
- K₂HPO₄ (dipotassium phosphate)
- FeCl₃
- Agar powder (only for solid form)
- DI water

## Procedure (Variant A — notebook 5-21)

> [!important] Label as you prepare
> Label every bottle and tube with its **substance, volume, and date** (e.g. `PMM — 500 mL — 2026-06-08`) before it goes on a shelf or into the fridge.

1. Add 900 mL DI water to a 1 L flask.
2. Dissolve **2.07 g NaH₂PO₄·H₂O** and **2.61 g K₂HPO₄** to form the phosphate buffer.
3. Adjust pH to **6.8** using 1 N NaOH or HCl.
4. Add **0.05 g FeCl₃** as mineral base; swirl to dissolve.
5. For solid medium, add **15 g agar** for solidification.
6. Separate into 2 bottles before autoclaving (notebook 5-21 explicit note).
7. Cap each bottle loosely with autoclave tape (Autoclave Indicator Tape) over the cap, then autoclave for **45 min**. (Liquid cycle.)
8. After autoclave, dispense into Petri dishes.
   - Notebook 5-21: **7 mL per plate** (made 40 plates from 1 L).
   - Notebook 5-22: **12 mL per plate**. `[TBD: standardize one volume.]`
9. While plates solidify, turn on UV light in the safety cabin to keep sterile (notebook 5-22).
