# Media Disposal

## Purpose
Inactivate liquid microbiological waste (used enrichment broths, spent media, leftover bacterial cultures) and dispose of it safely down the sink. Used at end-of-day cleanup, and specifically when retiring soil-enrichment flasks after their material has been recovered (e.g., after [[phage_isolation]] is complete).

## Materials & equipment
- Bleach (`[TBD: working concentration — notebook 6-4 just says "bleaching solution"; standard practice is 10 % household bleach or 1 % sodium hypochlorite]`)
- Sink with DI water flush
- Gloves, lab coat, eye protection

## Reagents
- Bleach (working concentration). `[TBD: concentration — 6-4 says only "bleaching solution"; standard practice is 10 % household bleach or 1 % sodium hypochlorite.]`
- DI water (for rinse)

## Procedure
1. **Add bleaching solution to the waste flask** with the liquid still in it.
2. **Let it sit for the bleach contact time** (`[TBD: minimum contact time — not documented in 6-4]`; commonly 10–30 min).
3. **After bleach contact time and after the day's work is done**, pour the contents of each flask into the **sink**.
4. **Wash the empty flasks** in the sink.
5. **Flush the flasks through with DI water** to remove residual bleach and detergent.

## Notes / common deviations
- The 6-4 notebook also notes the team uses this protocol specifically to **retire soil-enrichment flasks** once the phage filtrate has been recovered: "Since we've had phage & bacteria from the soil enrichment, we no longer need those 6 flasks and dispose it."
- For solid-waste (plates, contaminated tips): follow local biohazard waste guidance — this protocol covers **liquid** disposal only.

- [ ] Bleached liquid waste → **sink** (after the bleach contact time).

- [ ] Bleach bottle → return to its storage location, cap tight.
